ٱلْأَمَانَة

How the times stay right.

A masjid moves its iqamah with the season, and it rarely announces that twice. Keeping up with it, for every masjid, is most of the work in this app, and it happens whether or not anyone notices.

Read again every week

Every masjid’s schedule is read again each week from whatever that masjid publishes. When a committee moves a time, the app follows, without waiting for anyone to report it.

Checked against the sun

Before a schedule goes live it is checked against the position of the sun at that masjid’s own coordinates. A time that could not be right for that place on that day is held back for a person to look at, rather than shown to you.

The check never invents

That check only ever catches times that are wrong. It never fills a gap, and it never decides an iqamah, because only a masjid can do that.

A gap is left as a gap

Where a masjid has not published its times, the app says so plainly and offers to pass yours on. An estimate in that space would send someone to an empty hall.

The community has the last word

If a time is wrong, or your masjid is missing, you can send the whole schedule from inside the app in a few taps: every prayer, adhan and iqamah, and Jumu’ah.

Which reaches the rest

Those corrections are what keep the directory honest, and they reach the masjids no automated reading can follow.

Good to know.

Is The’Salat really free?

Yes, free forever. No ads, no subscriptions, no in-app purchases, and no accounts. It is built as a sadaqah jariyah for the community.

Why the apostrophe in the name?

It is not decoration. “The Salat” and “TheSalat” were both already taken in App Store search, so the name carries one mark in order to be found. On your home screen the app simply reads TheSalat.

How are the prayer times verified?

Every masjid’s schedule is read again each week from whatever that masjid publishes, and checked against the position of the sun at that masjid’s own coordinates before it goes live. A time that could not be right for that place on that day is held back for a person to look at. Nothing is ever calculated in place of an iqamah, and if a time still looks off, one tap in the app sends us a correction.

My masjid isn’t listed. Can I add it?

Absolutely. When a masjid near you does not have times yet, it still appears in the app, and a single tap lets you send its whole schedule: every prayer, adhan and iqamah, and Jumu’ah. We add it, in shā’ Allāh.

Do you track my location or my prayers?

Never. Your location is used on your device to find nearby masjids, and is shared only with Apple Maps to estimate drive time. Nothing about you or your worship is stored or profiled. The app works even if you decline location, though the widget needs it before it can tell you when to leave.

Which devices are supported?

The’Salat is exclusively for iPhone, built natively for iOS, with a home screen widget in two sizes for the next jama’ah. An iPhone-first experience means it stays fast, light, and at home on your device.
